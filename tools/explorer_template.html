02

Two questions, and no test data yet

Q1

What loads does the blanket put on the mechanism?

Force at the root and tip attachments over time, especially the peak when the blanket snaps taut.

Q2

What loads does the blanket take itself?

Tension along the film, fold angles, and cell curvature checked against the allowable bend radius of the silicon strips.

Cells are stiff along their strips and flexible across the gaps, so the blanket is modelled as stiff panels joined by flexible fold lines that remember their stowed shape. The mechanism itself is represented only by the motion it imposes.

Every material value is a placeholder. Today's job is how loads scale and what to measure first, not final numbers.

08

A ground test in air will not see the in-orbit snap

Air drag alone removes most of the in-orbit peak by damping the flapping that drives the whip.

≈ 174N/m

With gravity, the ground load is simply the sag tension of the deployed blanket, not a dynamic peak.

To validate the in-orbit load case, plan at least one vacuum or reduced-pressure deployment and a gravity offload.

09

Every run bends the cells past the placeholder limit

Each fold springs back and pushes on the panel beside it. Moment over bending stiffness gives the curvature at the panel edge, before any dynamics:

κ · Rmin ≈ khinge(π − θrest) · Rmin / EI
= 0.026 × 0.7π × 0.5 / 0.01
≈ 2.9 × allowable (3.3 simulated)
EI ≳ 0.03N·m

Panel stiffness needed to stay within the allowable with placeholder fold properties: about 3× the placeholder.

Whether this is real depends on two unmeasured things: the fold moment–angle curve and panel EI.

Deployment speed

The biggest lever of all (×9–10 between 7.5 s and 120 s) is a control choice, not an unknown. Exercise several speeds in every deployment test, with a load cell at the attachment.

Limits of this model

Planar strip; no contact between stack layers or stowage box; hinges are points; air is drag only. Curvature right beside a hinge is resolution-sensitive in fast snaps.
